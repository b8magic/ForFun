- **DO NOT PUBLISH** means it must not appear on the site in any form.

### E2. DECISION: omit, and say nothing legal

**Website rule:** the Fuego extra hike does **not** appear anywhere on the site. That covers:
- tour cards, prices, "optional extras" boxes, badges and itinerary lines
- the FAQ, the booking widget and prefilled WhatsApp texts
- image captions, alt text, meta and OG text
- the R2 review sentence "Did Acatenango / Fuego…"
- photos of people on Fuego's ridge, saddle or flank
- captioning `942c4bdf` (hiker and dog on a black-ash slope) as a Fuego route

**Also no legal wording:** no mention of laws, bans, permits, CONRED, INSIVUMEH, CONAP or INGUAT on the site. This matches CD.

**Why this overrides CD's Fuego directive and report 06's add-on copy:**
1. **Safety of the readers.** The site would invite international travellers into a zone that the national volcano institute, as of 31 Oct 2026, names as prohibited because of incandescent ballistics. Someone died there in 2024.
2. **Legal exposure for Alexander.** The park administrator's total ban explicitly covers agencies and guides (1 Apr 2026). INGUAT (10 Oct 2026) says organising or selling trips to prohibited zones can bring administrative and criminal liability. The June 2026 joint statement asks the public to report agencies that sell these trips. A public English price list would be documentary evidence.
3. **Omitting the hike also satisfies CD's goal.** Nothing on the page needs a legal disclaimer if the page doesn't sell the hike. Alexander keeps full freedom to talk with guests in person, which is his responsibility.
4. **Conversion does not need it.** The camp's selling point is that Fuego is straight ahead, so no extra hike is needed to see it. Five reviews praise the view; none praises the Fuego hike.

**Exact recommended wording:** none. Omit the topic.
