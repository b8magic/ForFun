# Judge: Conversion & Trust (CRO lens)

**Lens:** I'm a CRO lead who has optimised WhatsApp-first tour businesses in Latin America. The buyer is an international traveller in Antigua, on a phone, often the night before, comparing hostel-resold tours that look the same. For a tiny operator with **no reviews on the site yet**, the bottleneck is trust: "Is this a real person, will he deliver, and how do I start?" Clarity and low-friction WhatsApp entry come next. Differentiation decides between otherwise equal options.

**Method:** I looked at every screenshot. That means all four first screens (mobile and desktop), and the full-page captures sliced into readable tiles. I also looked at the extra state captures for the dock, the sheet, the pass and the seat toggles. I read every concept.md. I grepped each prototype for its CTA targets, no-JS fallbacks, WhatsApp templates, FAQ answers (payment, Fuego) and unsourced claims.

**Scores (1–10):**

**Winner for conversion & trust: B, "Hecho en La Soledad".**

## A · Front Row to Fuego (24-hour film): 7.2

**Strengths**
- The first mobile screen works. It has the strongest hero photo (the red plume), a clear H1 ("Front-row seats to an erupting volcano"), "Overnight · all included Q750 ≈ US$100 per person", a WhatsApp-glyph CTA, and three chips (2–8 per guide · Bed at camp · 3 meals + transport).
- The seat map (front row vs side seat, "Schematic, not to scale") makes the left/right camp argument in about 3 seconds. The theatre metaphor fits this one claim perfectly.
- The ticket builder is good. It shows a live total in Q and US$, extras, a Panajachel option and a sticky "Send on WhatsApp | Q2,250" button in the sheet. The message ends with "Website price: Q750 per person (Q2,250 total)", which is the best price-anchoring line of the four because it prevents disputes later. "Nothing is charged here" sits under the button.
- Honesty touches are good: a "Straight talk" card on the guides' English, "approx." on times, dashed review slots and "Mountains make their own plans."

**Weaknesses**
- **Metaphor load.** "COLD OPEN", the HUD pill "Day 1 21:40", "Box office", "Wardrobe department", "From the audience", "Post-credits scene", "Fin." Many travellers in Antigua read English as a second language (European and Israeli backpackers), and this jargon costs them comprehension. The dock's left half shows "Day 1 · 14:00", which tells the buyer nothing.
- **The offer arrives late.** The full included list and the box office sit around screen 13 of about 21 on mobile. This is the longest page of the four (about 17.5k CSS px), with big empty gaps between scenes. The dock rescues it, but a night-before buyer who scrolls to check what's included waits a long time.
- **The concept's spine is unconfirmed data.** Giant numerals for 08:00 pickup, 17:45, 04:30 and 06:00 are "illustrative" by the designer's own admission. Pickup is still in conflict (8:00 vs 6:30). If it's 6:30, the opening scene of the story is wrong.
- The cinematic polish reads like a well-funded agency. That works against the key differentiator ("small local operator, not a big agency"). Alexander gets quotes but no presence.
- The FAQ has no "How do I pay?" item.

## B · Hecho en La Soledad (Made by locals): 8.6, WINNER

**Strengths**
- **The clearest value proposition in five words.** "Sleep facing Fuego." The kicker "Acatenango · 2 days, 1 night" carries the rest. The lede "Family-run at the Acatenango trailhead… 2–8 hikers per guide, and your cabin faces Fuego" covers who, how many and why. The price box (Q750, per person ≈ US$100, "transport, guide, entry, cabin & 3 meals") and the CTA are all above the fold at 390×844.
- **The best CTA wording for this buyer.** "Check a date on WhatsApp" is the lowest-commitment phrasing, and it matches the night-before question ("is there space tomorrow?"). The red button with white text (7.1:1) is unmistakable. The microcopy "You write to Alexander directly. No booking office in between." is trust and differentiation in one line at the point of action. On desktop, the "¡Hola! I'm Alexander" taped note above the H1 is the strongest trust device in the set.
- **Trust without reviews.** This is what a no-review local operator needs, and B does it best:
  - a named human and a lined-paper note "translated from his voice notes"
  - a **"What we won't promise"** card (clear skies, a private cabin, fluent English)
  - "Our honest bit: we can promise the seat, not the eruption"
  - the "What we tell you is what we give you" stamp
  - "Real footage from one of our cabins. No staging, no filter."
  - "Gracias for supporting community guides"

  Saying the weak points out loud makes the strong claims believable.
- **Objection handling is complete and in the right places.** "What you bring" sits right beside the ticket. "Getting there" has Open-in-Maps pins and the luggage line "Things don't go missing here." The FAQ covers English, a private cabin, clouds, the suitcase, difficulty, Fuego and **"How do I pay?"**.
- **The differentiation is shown with proof.** The sightline toggle (usual camps vs our camp) comes with **"One view, three shows"**: the same view through the cabin-door frame in real afternoon, sunset and after-dark photos. That proves "no extra hike for sunset" better than any diagram. The loom (60 ghost figures vs 8 + 1 guide) makes "small groups" visible at a glance.
- **A good booking flow.** The "Note to Alexander" sheet has date + flexible, a stepper with guide count (a second red guide appears at 9), pickup radios, extras and an optional name. It shows a **live preview of the WhatsApp message**, and the message ends with an **(ES) Spanish summary line** for Alexander. The Send bar is sticky. There is a **"Just a question? Chat without the form"** escape hatch and the second number. The message asks Alexander to "confirm availability and the pickup time", which handles the 8:00/6:30 conflict gracefully.
- **Fuego is handled correctly.** It appears in one FAQ answer only, with no price and an explicit "set by the authorities, rules change, only what's allowed, never paid in advance". This is the only safe treatment besides A's.
- The dock is the clearest of the four: "Overnight · Q750 · per person ≈ US$100 | Check a date". It hides at the booking section.

**Weaknesses**
- The WhatsApp message and the slip show **no group total**. Only "Q750 pp" appears.
- **"Family-run"** (hero lede, woven ribbon) is **not in Alexander's words**. He says "local agency" and "I welcome you at my house". This is an unverified claim in a concept built on "every claim is something they can show you."
- "Morning pickup; Alexander confirms the exact time" is too vague for a buyer deciding whether they can make it.
- The tour selector in the slip is a native `<select>`. It takes one more tap and hides the start times and prices.
- On mobile, the logo patch over the hero arch duplicates the header logo and competes with the headline. The "You write to Alexander directly" line falls just below the fold.
- The hero kicker doesn't say "from Antigua". Transport only appears inside the price note.

## C · Summit Pass (ticket booth): 7.8

**Strengths**
- **The best price presentation.** "Q750 · US$100 per person, all in" is huge and appears in the hero stub. "One price. Everything up the mountain." The pass computes totals in GTQ and USD.
- **The strongest differentiation devices.** The Seat L / Seat R illustrated viewfinder sits next to a real "Real photo · Solex camp" image. A scannable **"Big Antigua tour vs Solex"** table compares group, pace, camp, sunset, extras and promise, with an honest footnote. A marquee repeats the proof points. A "Max 8 per guide" stamp sits on the hero.
- **The best sticky dock.** Once the pass has data, it rewrites itself into a live booking summary ("SUN 8 NOV · 3 PPL · Q2,250 · US$300 → Send pass"). This is excellent for people who fill in the form, scroll away and come back.
- The 24-hour itinerary sits beside the offer card, so price and schedule are in one view. The message includes an estimated total and "8:00 AM (please confirm)" with bilingual labels.

**Weaknesses**
- **CTA friction.** "Build your Summit Pass" is invented jargon ("what's a Summit Pass?") with an effort verb ("build"). The hero CTA is an anchor to `#build` and jumps about 10 screens down to a 6-step form instead of opening a sheet in place. The real WhatsApp option is a secondary text link.
- **The jargon leaks to the owner.** The message opens "I'd like to book a Summit Pass" with "Pass: SLX-OVN-0811-03". Alexander has no such product or reference system, and a traveller may read the "pass number" as a confirmation.
- **Fuego risk.** "Extra hike toward Fuego… Q300, paid at camp" is printed on the main offer card. The VS table's "Extras: Paid upfront, no refund / Decide at camp, pay there" row is effectively about the Fuego hike. The FAQ prices it too. Fuego ascents are restricted by the authorities, so this is a trust and liability problem, not a small add-on.
- The loud poster voice reads as a promo booth rather than a family at the trailhead, which is less intimate than the story deserves. The placeholder heading "Reviews go here. Real ones only." must not ship. The page also says "family-run" (unsourced).

## D · CONTOUR (survey sheet): 8.2

**Strengths**
- **The most information-complete first screen.** The kicker reads "Acatenango 3,976 m / **Overnight from Antigua**" (the only hero that says "from Antigua"). The H1 is "Fuego, straight ahead." A real photo carries a crosshair, a "FUEGO 3,763 m · Active volcano" label and a **"Real photo · Night eruption"** tag. Below come the price, four inclusions, a full-width "Check your date on WhatsApp" button and "Pickup in Antigua · Café Condesa or Café La Parada". A six-item legend follows directly.
- **The single best at-a-glance differentiator in the set:** the field note "~~35–60~~ → **2–8** per Solex guide", headed "No herding. No rushing." There is also the sightline viewfinder and the legend.
- **A smart micro-conversion.** The group gauge stepper ends in "Ask for 2 people", which opens the manifest pre-filled. Every `wa.me` link has prefilled text even without JS, the best no-JS fallback of the four.
- The manifest is excellent. It has radio cards with start time and price, a people stepper showing "1 guide · Q1,500 total (≈US$200)", "After the climb", rentals, a live preview, bilingual labels, a sticky "Open WhatsApp" button and an "Other line" link that carries the same text. The FAQ is the most complete and leads with "What time is pickup, and where?". It includes "How do I book?".

**Weaknesses**
- **Precision theatre.** The live altimeter shows exact interpolated figures ("ALT 2,690 m"), with "Camp ≈3,600 m" and "La Soledad ≈2,400 m" as estimates, all on a survey-precise visual system. The credibility of the style becomes a liability if a number is wrong. The dock spends its left half on this decorative readout instead of the offer.
- **An invented claim.** "Our guides grew up here, and **many spent years working for the big agencies**" has no source. Only Alexander did. "Family agency" and "money stays in La Soledad" are also unconfirmed.
- **Fuego risk.** "+Q300 per person" sits in the spec sheet's Optional list, and the FAQ adds "Nothing to prepay, nothing to lose". There is no regulatory caveat. Same problem as C.
- On mobile the sticky route strip at the top plus the dock at the bottom take about 170 px of an 844 px screen. "Fuego, straight ahead." on its own needs the lede to make sense to a first-timer. The cool Swiss tone is competent but less warm than B, and Alexander only appears through quotes.

## Winner: B, "Hecho en La Soledad"

B turns the brand's real asset, a named local man who tells you the truth, into the conversion engine. It also keeps the offer, price and WhatsApp action on the first screen. D is a close second on clarity and differentiation, and C has the best price display and dock. Their best mechanics can be grafted onto B. B's trust architecture cannot be grafted onto them without changing their voice.

### Grafts onto B (concrete)
1. **From D:** make the loom's headline the strike-through numeral "~~35–60~~ → **2–8** hikers per guide". Add D's **"Ask for N people"** button under B's loom stepper, sharing state with the "Note to Alexander" slip.
2. **From D:** change the hero kicker to "**Overnight from Antigua** · 2 days, 1 night". Add the line "Pickup in Antigua · Café Condesa or Café La Parada" under the hero CTA. Tag the proof photos "Real photo · from our camp" (once confirmed).
3. **From D:** give every `wa.me` link a prefilled text (no-JS fallback), including the header and dock. Reorder the FAQ to lead with "What time is pickup, and where?" and add "How do I book?".
4. **From C:** a dock that **turns into the live booking summary** once the slip has data ("Sun 8 Nov · 3 people · Q2,250 → Send"). The dock currently says "Overnight · Q750 | Check a date".
5. **From C:** a short, soft **"Most Antigua tours / Solex"** comparison (group size · camp side · sunset · what's included) right after the loom, keeping C's footnote "what Alexander saw in his years guiding; not every agency works like this". Leave out the Extras/Fuego row.
6. **From C:** on desktop, a condensed "Your 24 hours" timeline beside the "Our word" ticket, so price and schedule are in one view.
7. **From A:** the message line "**Website price: Q750 per person (Q1,500 total)**" to anchor the price, plus "Nothing is charged here" microcopy under Send.
8. **From A, C and D:** replace the slip's `<select>` with **radio cards** showing each tour's start time and price. Optionally add A's hero chips (2–8 per guide · Bed at camp · 3 meals + transport) beside the price box for the fastest scan.

### Must-fix in B
1. **Add a group total** (Q and ≈US$) to the slip, the preview, the dock and the WhatsApp message. Today the message carries only "Q750 pp".
2. **Remove or verify "Family-run"** (hero lede, woven ribbon, meta). The source only supports "local agency, run from Alexander's house at the trailhead".
3. **Pickup time:** settle 8:00 vs 6:30 with Alexander, then show "around 8:00 AM, confirmed on WhatsApp" on the ticket and in the FAQ. "Morning pickup" is too vague for a night-before decision.
4. **Mobile hero:** drop or shrink the logo patch that overlaps the arch (it duplicates the header logo and competes with the H1). Pull "You write to Alexander directly…" above the fold. Add "from Antigua" to the kicker.
5. **"How do I pay?"** is a non-answer ("Alexander explains when he confirms"). Get the real method (cash at pickup or trailhead? deposit? transfer?) and the cancellation and weather rule. Payment uncertainty is the top objection in WhatsApp bookings because travellers fear transfer scams.
6. **Reviews:** never ship the visible "PLACEHOLDER" cards. Fill them with real, linked Google reviews or hide the section. Meanwhile add a link to the Google Maps listing and the Instagram feed beside the ticket as third-party proof.
7. **Confirm the photo claims:** the hero annotation "the view from our cabins" and the door-frame "three shows" photos must have been taken at the Solex camp.
8. **Legibility:** the Caveat hand-notes in sun-yellow over bright sky (hero) need a contrast check. Keep the hand font out of anything actionable.
9. **The Spanish (ES) line:** keep it, but make it include the date, people, pickup **and the total**, so Alexander can confirm from the preview alone.

### Notes for whichever concept ships
- No concept states a cancellation or weather policy. Get it from Alexander, because it is a decisive objection for a weather-dependent overnight.
- Keep the Fuego extension at A's or B's level (FAQ only, no price, authorities' rules stated) until the legal check is done. C and D need this fix if any of their offer cards are reused.
