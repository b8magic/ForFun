A note from Alexander

“If you pay for a tour, it’s so you can enjoy it, without being rushed.”

I started out as a guide on Acatenango. They used to send us up with groups of 35, 40, sometimes 60 people, and very few guides. The slow walkers got hurried along. The fast ones got frustrated.

So we went out on our own: a local agency at the main entrance to the volcano, with guides from the area and small groups, so people can really enjoy Acatenango.

Some agencies promise you all sorts of things, and then, when you actually take the tour, it’s different. With us, what we tell you we’ll provide is what we give you. If you need anything, write to me. I’m here.

AlexanderLa Soledad, main entrance to Acatenango

Where you sleep matters

We camp on the right side. Fuego is straight ahead.

Many groups from Antigua camp on the left-hand side, looking toward Antigua and Guatemala City with Fuego off to one side. From there you catch the big eruptions but can miss the small ones, because most of the lava runs down the side we face.

“Other agencies don’t have that view from their camps. That’s why they offer a separate walk to see the sunset.”

Alexander, translated from Spanish

How the two days go

From a café in Antigua to the summit at sunrise.

  1. Day 1

    ~8:00 AM · Antigua

    Pickup at the café

    Café Condesa (Parque Central) or Café La Parada (Parque La Merced). Bring your suitcase; it rides with us.

    ~9:00 AM · La Soledad

    Alexander opens his door

    About an hour’s drive. Bathroom, instructions, rent gear if you need it, meet your guide. Leave your luggage: “Things don’t go missing here.” Keep your passport, money and phone with you.

  2. Midday

    The climb

    At your group’s pace. Lunch on day 1 is included. Nobody gets hurried along.

  3. Afternoon

    Camp, right side

    Your cabin is ready: mattress, sleeping bag, pillow. Fuego is straight ahead.

  4. Sunset

    Sunset from camp

    No extra hike, as long as it isn’t cloudy.

  5. Night

    Dinner, hot chocolate, and Fuego

    On clear nights you see the small eruptions as well as the big ones. It gets cold: layers on.

  6. Day 2

    Before dawn

    About an hour to 3,976 m

    Leave your things at camp and climb by headlamp to the summit of Acatenango.

  7. Sunrise

    Daybreak from the top

    The volcanoes line up along the horizon on clear mornings.

  8. Morning

    Back to camp

    Coffee, breakfast, pack up.

    Descent

    Down to La Soledad

    Collect your luggage at Alexander’s house.

    Return

    Same café in Antigua

    Or ask about heading on toward Panajachel: it can save you about 40 minutes.

Before you go

What to bring, and how hard it is.

Pack

  • 4 litres of water per person: our golden rule.
  • Extra warm clothes: hat, gloves, a warm layer. Nights at camp are cold.
  • A day pack for your things.
  • Headlamp and good shoes (or rent them).
  • Bring your passport — it’s checked at the park entrance.
  • Your suitcase can stay at Alexander’s house.

Weather

Views need clear skies, and clouds come and go. Sunset, eruptions and the summit view all depend on the weather on the day. We can promise the seat facing Fuego, not the show.

Fitness

It’s a steep climb at altitude, up to the 3,976 m summit. You walk at your group’s pace with 2–8 people per guide, so nobody gets rushed. Not sure? Ask Alexander on WhatsApp before you book.

Before you book

Honest answers.

Something else? Ask Alexander on WhatsApp

What time is pickup, and where?

Around 8:00 AM at Café Condesa (Parque Central) or Café La Parada (Parque La Merced) in Antigua. Alexander confirms the exact time on WhatsApp.

How do I book?

Tap “Check a date on WhatsApp”, fill in what you know, and send the message. Alexander replies personally and confirms your spot. Nothing is booked or charged on this website.

How do I pay?

Alexander confirms how to pay when he confirms your spot on WhatsApp. Nothing is charged on this website.

Do the guides speak English?

Mainly Spanish and a little English. They’re from the area, warm and very entertaining, and their job is to make sure you leave happy.

Will we see Fuego erupt?

From our camp Fuego is straight ahead, so on clear nights you can see small eruptions as well as big ones. But the clouds and the volcano decide; nobody can promise a show.

Will we get a private cabin?

Cabins sleep six and are shared. If it’s a quiet night, two of you may get one to yourselves. Alexander tells you closer to the date.

What if it’s cloudy?

Nobody can promise the weather. As Alexander says, “We have those views when the skies are clear.” Ask him on WhatsApp what happens on a cloudy or rainy day.

Can I leave my suitcase?

Yes. Bring it on the shuttle and leave it at Alexander’s house at the main entrance while you hike. In his words: “Things don’t go missing here.” Keep your passport, money and phone with you.

How hard is the hike?

It’s a steep climb at altitude, up to the 3,976 m summit. You walk at your group’s pace with 2–8 people per guide, so nobody gets rushed. Not sure? Ask Alexander on WhatsApp before you book.

What should I bring?

4 litres of water per person and extra warm clothes. Any gear you’re missing, you can rent from us. Bring your passport: it’s checked at the park entrance. See the packing list.

Can I go straight on to Lake Atitlán?

Yes, ask about heading on toward Panajachel on the way back. As Alexander puts it, “you don’t have to go all the way into Antigua”; it can save you about 40 minutes.

Can we get closer to Fuego?

Yes, if you want to. From camp there’s an optional extra hike toward Fuego, then back to the same camp to sleep. It adds about 4 hours, sometimes more, and costs Q300 per person. You decide at camp, if you feel fit and the weather is right, and pay there. Nothing is charged in advance, so you lose nothing if you don’t go.

Can I write in English?

Yes, write in English or Spanish. Our form also adds a short Spanish summary for Alexander.
